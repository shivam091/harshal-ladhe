## Introduction

Knockout text is text **cut out of a solid shape**, so whatever sits behind the shape shows through the letters: a photo, a gradient, a pattern or the page itself. A `<clipPath>` can only keep shapes, not cut holes in them, but an SVG `<mask>` can do both.

The recipe is short: a white rectangle in the mask keeps the shape visible, black text in the mask hides it where the letters are, and the letters become holes.

## The Code

<SvgSnippet>
{`
  <svg viewBox="0 0 260 100" width="390" height="150" role="img" aria-label="SALE">
    <defs>
      <linearGradient id="ko-backdrop" x1="0" y1="0" x2="1" y2="1">
        <stop offset="0" stop-color="#6366f1" />
        <stop offset="0.5" stop-color="#ec4899" />
        <stop offset="1" stop-color="#f59e0b" />
      </linearGradient>
      <mask id="ko-mask" maskUnits="userSpaceOnUse" x="20" y="15" width="220" height="70">
        <rect x="20" y="15" width="220" height="70" fill="white" />
        <text x="130" y="63" font-family="system-ui, sans-serif" font-size="40" font-weight="800" text-anchor="middle" fill="black">SALE</text>
      </mask>
    </defs>
    <!-- Backdrop: anything behind the plate shows through the letters -->
    <rect width="260" height="100" rx="12" fill="url(#ko-backdrop)" />
    <rect x="20" y="15" width="220" height="70" rx="8" fill="currentColor" mask="url(#ko-mask)" />
  </svg>
`}
</SvgSnippet>

<RainbowDivider />

## How It Works

1. The gradient `<rect>` is only a backdrop for the demo. It stands in for whatever is behind the knockout in a real design.
2. The solid plate is a `<rect>` filled with `currentColor`, so it's dark on a light page and light on a dark one.
3. Inside the `<mask>`, a **white** `<rect>` the same size as the plate means "show everything". The mask is a luminance mask by default, so white is fully visible.
4. The **black** `<text>` on top of it means "hide here". Wherever the letters are, the plate becomes fully transparent and the gradient behind shows through.
5. `maskUnits="userSpaceOnUse"` with `x`, `y`, `width` and `height` sets the mask region to exactly the plate, in the same coordinates as the drawing. The text is centred with `text-anchor="middle"` at the plate's centre, `x="130"`.

## Variant: Punching Out Any Shape

The text can be any shape. Black shapes, strokes, or several of them together, all become holes. Here a check mark is cut out of a circle badge, over a striped pattern:

<SvgSnippet>
{`
  <svg viewBox="0 0 100 100" width="120" height="120" role="img" aria-label="Done">
    <defs>
      <pattern id="ko-stripes-2" width="10" height="10" patternUnits="userSpaceOnUse" patternTransform="rotate(45)">
        <rect width="5" height="10" fill="#14b8a6" />
      </pattern>
      <mask id="ko-mask-2" maskUnits="userSpaceOnUse" x="0" y="0" width="100" height="100">
        <rect width="100" height="100" fill="white" />
        <polyline points="34,51 45,62 67,40" fill="none" stroke="black" stroke-width="8" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" />
      </mask>
    </defs>
    <rect width="100" height="100" rx="16" fill="url(#ko-stripes-2)" />
    <circle cx="50" cy="50" r="36" fill="currentColor" mask="url(#ko-mask-2)" />
  </svg>
`}
</SvgSnippet>

The same pattern gives you a spotlight overlay with a round hole, a card with a notch, or a badge with an icon punched through it. Grey shapes in the mask make semi-transparent holes, and blurred ones make soft-edged holes.

## Example Usage

Put the SVG over any HTML background and the background shows through the letters. To make it fill its container at any size, size the `<svg>` with CSS. The `viewBox` scales everything inside it, including the mask and the text:

<SvgSnippet>
{`
  <div class="ko-hero">
    <svg class="ko-hero-title" viewBox="0 0 300 80" width="300" height="80" role="img" aria-label="Summer Sale">
      <defs>
        <mask id="ko-mask-3" maskUnits="userSpaceOnUse" x="0" y="0" width="300" height="80">
          <rect width="300" height="80" fill="white" />
          <text x="150" y="51" font-family="system-ui, sans-serif" font-size="32" font-weight="800" text-anchor="middle" fill="black">SUMMER SALE</text>
        </mask>
      </defs>
      <rect width="300" height="80" rx="10" fill="#0f172a" mask="url(#ko-mask-3)" />
    </svg>
  </div>

  <style>
    .ko-hero {
      inline-size: min(100%, 360px);
      padding: 1.5rem;
      border-radius: 1rem;
      background: linear-gradient(120deg, #f59e0b, #ec4899, #6366f1, #14b8a6);
    }

    .ko-hero-title {
      display: block;
      inline-size: 100%;
      block-size: auto;
    }
  </style>
`}
</SvgSnippet>

Swap the gradient for `background-image: url(photo.jpg)` and the photo shows through the letters instead.

## Use Cases

- **Hero headings** with a photo or video showing through the letters.
- **Badges and stickers** with an icon or number punched out.
- **Onboarding spotlights**, a dark overlay with a hole over the feature being introduced.
- **Logos and wordmarks** that need to sit on changing backgrounds.

## Notes & Tips

- **Label it:** text inside a `<mask>` is never rendered or exposed to assistive technology, so screen readers don't see "SALE" at all. Add `role="img"` and an `aria-label` with the same words on the `<svg>`. The text also can't be selected, translated or found with find-in-page, so keep knockouts to short headings.
- **Leave room for the font:** the text is measured with whatever font loads. A fallback font can be wider than the one you designed with, so leave some space on each side of the plate.
- **Why not `objectBoundingBox` units?** With `maskContentUnits="objectBoundingBox"`, `font-size` becomes a fraction of the shape and the letters stretch with its proportions. For text, keep the mask in user space and let the `viewBox` handle scaling.
- **Unique ids:** each mask is referenced by id, and the first match in the document wins. If you render the same inline SVG more than once, give each copy its own id.
- **Masks don't change hit-testing:** the holes are still part of the plate for the pointer. Links or buttons behind the plate can't be clicked through the letters unless you set `pointer-events: none` on the SVG.
- **Filled letters instead of holes:** if you want the letters to be the only visible part, filled with a gradient, use CSS `background-clip: text`. See the [CSS gradient text guide](/post/css/css-gradient-text).
- **Browser support:** SVG `<mask>` inside an `<svg>` works in all current browsers.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<Details summary="Why white and black in the mask?">
  An SVG mask is a luminance mask by default. White means fully visible and black means fully hidden. The white rect
  keeps the whole plate, and the black text removes the plate wherever the letters are, leaving holes.
</Details>

<Details summary="Why is the whole shape invisible?">
  A mask starts empty, and anything not painted counts as hidden. Check that the white rect covers the shape in the
  same coordinates, and that the mask region (`x`, `y`, `width`, `height` on the `<mask>`) covers it too.
</Details>

<Details summary="How do I make it responsive?">
  Keep the `viewBox` and set the SVG's width with CSS, for example `inline-size: 100%` and `block-size: auto`. The
  mask, the plate and the text all scale together, because they share the SVG's coordinate system.
</Details>

<Details summary="Can I use a web font?">
  Yes. Set `font-family` on the `<text>` as usual. Until the font loads, the browser uses a fallback, so the letters may
  change width once it arrives. Leave some space around the text so neither font runs off the plate.
</Details>

<RainbowDivider />

## Key Takeaway

Put a white rect and black `<text>` inside a `<mask>`, apply it to a solid shape, and the letters become holes that show whatever is behind. Keep the mask in user space and let the `viewBox` scale it, and give the SVG `role="img"` and an `aria-label`, because the masked text itself isn't exposed.
